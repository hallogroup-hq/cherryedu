# Barista V2 — Experimentation & Curiosity Standard

**Status:** Pedagogical design only. No production feature, live data write or professional skill validation has been implemented.

## Core purpose

Build **experimental curiosity** into routine barista practice. The outcome is a barista who naturally asks "kenapa?" and "bagaimana kalau?", forms a useful hypothesis, tests within safe boundaries, learns from an unexpected outcome and can adapt when recipes, beans, tools or customer needs change.

Experiments are not synonymous with signature drinks. Changing only the grind, adjusting a filter brew ratio, trying a more suitable service explanation and revising a workflow can be as instructive as creating an original beverage. Curiosity is observable behavior, **not an innate trait we grade**.

## Seven-step experiment cycle

1. **Ask a question:** Learner explains what they are curious about and why it matters.
2. **Hypothesize:** Predict a possible causal mechanism and a testable direction of change.
3. **Design:** Name the independent variable, meaningful controls, outcomes and measurement tools. For complex questions, compare well-defined conditions and explicitly limit causal claims.
4. **Safety check:** Follow food safety, allergen protocols, manufacturer guidance, bar SOP and operator authorization; unsafe or unapproved tests are canceled/escalated.
5. **Try:** Perform a safe trial with actual equipment when available and appropriately supervised; otherwise interpret explicitly labeled synthetic data. No machine? No fake claim of having pulled a shot or tasted a drink.
6. **Observe and reflect:** Record actual measurements, sensory feedback where tasted, contradictory evidence, uncertainty and unexpected outcomes—**not just success stories**.
7. **Iterate:** Explain the next test, keep the result, revise the original idea or stop because the evidence/risk warrants it.

## Representative mini-labs — 12-module mapping

| Module | Experiment / question | Controls and safety |
|---|---|---|
| M01 | Does a station layout change safe order handoff? | Safe reach, clean/dirty zones, no speed competition |
| M02 | How do two brew-water ratios differ with one coffee? | Coffee dose, grinder, water, brew method and measured vs perceived outcomes |
| M03 | What changes when espresso beverage yield changes? | Stable dose/grind where practical; distinguish mass and taste |
| M04 | Why does a familiar shot vary when one condition changes? | Competing hypotheses, minimal safe tests, actual readings |
| M05 | How do controlled milk aeration changes affect texture? | Same milk, temperature safety, authorized wand use |
| M06 | What shifts taste if a beverage construction variable changes? | Defined recipe, no allergen improvisation |
| M07 | When should a service-filter recipe change for different goals? | Brewer, flow, dose and controlled variables |
| M08 | Which clarification question improves an unusual customer request? | Respectful evidence, privacy and allergy limits |
| M09 | Which user-safe inspection helps distinguish a fault before escalation? | Never open electrical/pressurized equipment |
| M10 | How do usable yield and waste influence unit cost? | Explicit money and quantity units |
| M11 | Does a new signature ingredient improve a defined sensory goal? | Hypothesis, blind trial where feasible, ingredients and food safety |
| M12 | Which next experiment is most informative under multiple failures? | Safety first, plausible causes, documented limits |

## True interactive teaching pattern

A real "Experiment Notebook" screen needs editable and distinct states: **Question → Prediction → Test Design → Results → Interpretation → Next Question**. A two-column text layout is NOT an interactive experiment. Show meaningful visible feedback only after effort; mark hypothetical outcomes as hypothetical. Let students choose alternatives and explain why; do not fake a universal recipe outcome or generate imaginary tasting reports.

Student-facing prompts should invite exploration rather than announce answers:
- "Penasaran apa yang terjadi kalau air ditambah tetapi ukuran giling tetap?"
- "Kamu menduga rasanya berubah karena apa? Bagaimana mengujinya?"
- "Prediksimu tidak terjadi. Informasi baru apa yang bisa kamu gunakan?"
- "Kalau hanya punya satu kesempatan percobaan lagi, apa yang paling ingin kamu ukur?"

**No-equipment route:** learner works with a synthetic table or video of a documented external experiment, states uncertainties, and chooses next test. Their result is assessed as *experimental reasoning*, not practical technique.

## Assessing the process rather than a lucky outcome

| Domain (formative) | What good evidence looks like |
|---|---|
| Question quality | Relevant, clear, actionable and connected to a real aim |
| Hypothesis and mechanism | Testable prediction with plausible physical/sensory reasoning; distinguishes assumption from fact |
| Experiment design | Meaningful variables, units, controls, feasible and safe procedure |
| Evidence integrity | Records actual vs simulated data, contradictions, incomplete information and variability |
| Learning from unexpected results | Revises or rejects hypothesis without pretending all trials succeed |
| Next-step judgment | Proposes an informative next test or correctly stops/escalates |
| Sharing and collaboration | Can explain the experiment and hand off results to a head barista or teammate |

**Do not award a high grade for recklessness, novelty without evidence, multiple changes that cannot be interpreted, or an invented tasting result.** Do not punish a cautious learner for stopping an unsafe trial. A validated passing score for these domains requires novice, trainer and qualified safety review.

## Practical experimentation without excessive cost

- Start with predictions and existing shot logs before consuming new materials.
- Use short batches, bounded variable changes, reusable sample data and only approved equipment.
- Prefer experiments during scheduled training/calibration rather than during a live rush when service safety could suffer.
- Use sensory sessions with appropriate hygiene and participant consent; document price, consumables and potential waste.
- Do not make possession of a refractometer or commercial espresso machine a prerequisite for completing the *online reasoning route*.

## Minimum QA before implementing

- [ ] Every skill module has at least one meaningful experiment plus reasoning transfer to a new scenario.
- [ ] The lesson includes a model of *why* a variable matters and at least one counterexample.
- [ ] No feedback reveals the hypothesis evaluation before the student attempts it.
- [ ] Learners can record unexpected or inconclusive findings without being marked wrong.
- [ ] No fixed ratio, grind, time or temperature is portrayed as a universally correct result.
- [ ] Units, assumptions and numerical boundaries of simulations are validated.
- [ ] Experiments are safe and explicitly scoped to the operator's authorization.
- [ ] Visual/video evidence truly shows the physical process when needed.
- [ ] Digital experiment notebooks preserve user work securely before making any promise of account persistence.
- [ ] Online simulated completion is never called proof of hands-on proficiency or formal certification.
