# CherryEdu Barista V2 — Competency Assessment & Independent Pilot Protocol

**Scope:** Curriculum design and pilot planning. **Not a BNSP/SCA examination, official assessor authorization, employment license, practical sign-off, or student graduation rule.**

## 1. What learning means here

A learner can demonstrate:
- **Conceptual understanding:** explain mechanisms, recipe variables, causes, tradeoffs and limitations.
- **Adaptive decision-making:** choose a safe response in a new bar scenario and revise when contradictory evidence appears.
- **Experimental literacy:** ask their own question, hypothesize, predict, control meaningful variables, record real vs simulated observations, and iterate honestly.
- **Operational readiness (only with real equipment and supervision):** execute safe espresso, milk, cleaning, service and a staff-reproducible signature drink.

The 36 changing-condition transfer cases, **24 misconception-focused multi-choice diagnostics** and 12 end-of-module checkpoints are **formative learning activities**, not authenticated or psychometrically validated exams. The 24 questions have explicit option-level explanations and do not count toward learner-account grade, credits or certificate. None proves real-world practical competence.

## 2. Competency mapping and suggested demonstrations

| Module | Independent novel-case test | Actual observable performance (supervised where relevant) | Safety-critical gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| M01 | Customer wording and changed hazard before open | Map real workstation and correctly hand off first order | Recognize allergen/steam/electric/wet risks and call stop |
| M02 | Explain 1:15 vs 1:16 and distinguish EY/TDS/bypass on unseen dataset | Plan a controlled, safe brew comparison using actual measured inputs | No fabricated tasting or reckless adjustments |
| M03 | Identify unknown grinder/basket parameters before copying 18→36 recipe | Set up authorized grinder/puck/portafilter and measure shots consistently | Do not touch moving burr, hot group or unauthorized settings |
| M04 | Analyze shot variability with 2+ hypotheses; choose discriminating tests | Conduct instructor-approved calibration, compare repeat shots and actual taste | Hold faulty machine and protect food-contact workflow |
| M05 | Identify wrong assumptions about foam/allergens | Observe and safely perform steaming, judge real milk textures | Critical allergen/cross-contact and burn-prevention steps |
| M06 | Adapt iced/milk recipes to actual brief; diagnose inaccurate tickets | Assemble two menu drinks, basic pour, check serve temp/ticket | Serve actual requested materials safely |
| M07 | Compare through-bed, immersion and bypass under queue constraints | Measure filter/batch output and repeat a venue-defined acceptable brew | Respect food holding and hot-water/equipment safe use |
| M08 | Handle unusual preferences and allergy reveal after recommendation | Neutral customer role-play and clear supervisor handoff | Never guarantee allergen-free without verified controls |
| M09 | Decide when to clean vs fault-escalate without dangerous intervention | User-authorized cleaning with correct label, tools and rinse | Stop abnormal electricity/pressure/steam; chemical handling |
| M10 | Solve FEFO vs unsafe batch; price with explicit unit/waste assumptions | Realistic stock labeling and HPP worksheet independently checked | Never use unverified ingredients to protect margin |
| M11 | Defend original concept under unexpected feedback and supplier change | Prototype original coffee-led drink, taste with consent, hand off to second barista, cost and refine | Allergen/storage risks independent critical holds |
| M12 | Integrate three changing conditions without following a prewritten solution | Supervised service shift and signature demo with transparent incident protocol | Any critical safety failure blocks practical approval |

**No universal single recipe required.** For any practical evaluation, venue-specific recipes, tolerances and equipment instructions must be approved by competent trainers. A learner can make a defensible alternative decision and should receive feedback on reasoning, not on conformity to an examiner's personal taste.

## 3. Four independent scoring domains (NOT yet calibrated)

| Domain | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Explanation | Can't identify relevant mechanism | Recalls isolated recipe/term | Explains causal variables and evidence | Explains mechanism, counterexample and uncertainty |
| Adaptation | Unsafe or arbitrary choice | Offers one prescription without context | Clarifies goals and chooses viable action | Evaluates multiple alternatives with tradeoffs |
| Experiment | Invents results or changes random parameters | States hypothesis with no test | Safe controls, measurable outcome | Good falsification, honest revision and next test |
| Communication | Confuses allergy/preference or hides constraints | Vague explanation | Clear handoff/clarification | Clear and respectful under new evidence and workload |

This is a **pilot rubric**, not a scientifically established pass score. The safety domain uses separate non-compensatory fail/hold rules. **Do not** compute a national/professional grade or declare 'competent' without the actual assessor, equipment, permissions and evaluation procedure.

## 4. Piloting with representative novices and practitioners

Recruit **6–10 novice learners** with varied prior coffee knowledge, literacy and phone comfort; additionally recruit **3–5 active baristas/head barista trainers** and an appropriate food-safety reviewer. Treat these as a proposed study size, not a statistical guarantee. Obtain meaningful consent for interviews and any recording, allow withdrawal, and protect potentially sensitive learner/workplace data.

Stages:
1. **Cold attempt** (no lesson): unfamiliar customer request + short recipe scenario; observe whether learner asks a clarifying question or merely supplies a ratio.
2. **Independent lesson use:** watch selected lesson without facilitator explanation. Observe when a person needs physical images or video, whether they confuse air-in vs beverage-out, and whether external safety expertise is required.
3. **Transfer with altered condition:** same mechanism but different bean/equipment/customer; observe explicit reasoning and contradictory evidence handling rather than answer recall.
4. **Experiment notebook:** learner invents a useful “what if?”, identifies variable, predicts, examines hypothetical/actual data and states a limitation.
5. **Scenario revision:** present new evidence only after decision. Compare original and revised answer; a different decision is not penalized merely for changing.
6. **Optional real skills session:** only with qualified instructor, safe equipment, equipment maker protocol, relevant policies and venue access; do not treat online completion as substitute.
7. **Debrief:** ask what felt forced, generic, repetitive, inaccessible, dangerous or merely text-based; gather concrete actions, not only a thumbs-up.
8. **Expert & editor sign-off:** verify coffee science, food safety, source/claim traceability, image rights, semantic UI and learner-facing copy.

Capture independently by lesson/case: completion attempt, misinterpretation type, missing prior knowledge, visual affordance failures, safety decisions, unexpected hypothesis revision, facilitator assistance needed, and improvement request. **No automatic pass percentage** without a justified evaluation protocol.

## 5. Inter-rater agreement and defect severity

Have at least two suitably trained reviewers score a subset of written responses independently. Reconcile where they disagree; look for prompts that appear to have one answer only because model guidance is vague. No multiple-choice pattern length analysis can validate essay reasoning by itself.

- **P0:** unsafe recipe/procedure; invented tasting; misleading credential/certification language; critical scientific error; answer accessible without real attempt when claiming integrity.
- **P1:** misordered prerequisite; pseudo-interaction; no physical visual for a motor skill; unclear units; fabricated outcome precision; shallow lesson preventing novice comprehension.
- **P2:** awkward copy, low-value decoration, formatting inconsistency, low contrast, repetitive headings or slow lesson navigation.

A lesson with P0 must not be shown as approved training. P1 needs documented fix and appropriate regression before release. A future edition update must preserve existing user progress and purchase rights.

## 6. Evidence ledger

Before a real release, a reviewer record must contain:
- Lesson ID and version hash; SME name/role, review date, claim or procedure reviewed, source edition and decision.
- Media asset ID, copyright owner/consent, accuracy sign-off, alt/caption/transcript evidence.
- Novice test result with question, changed-condition prompt and independently coded transfer observation.
- Safety check outcome separate from theory grade.
- Exam item bank review and proper assessment calibration. Current 36 changing-condition essays and 24 diagnostics are **practice**, not an official or high-stakes exam.
- Deployment copy/UI snapshot to ensure no release jargon or review status leaks into the learner view.
- Evidence no existing student purchase, progress, quiz attempts, or certificates were silently reset.
