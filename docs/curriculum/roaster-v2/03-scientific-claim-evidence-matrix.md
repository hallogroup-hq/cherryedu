# Roaster V2 — Scientific Evidence and Misconception Matrix

Purpose: reviewer-facing claim crosswalk and factual limits; **not** shown on student pages. Checked on 2026-10-13. Citation links are sources, not proof of CherryEdu SCA accreditation. All equipment operation, emergencies, cleaning and safety decisions follow the **specific installed model manual**, local regulations and trained supervisors.

## Evidence hierarchy and how to read it

1. Peer-reviewed sensory/chemistry studies are useful when they isolate variables, report sample type, roast degree and sensory methods; study findings **cannot** be copied as a temperature prescription across machines.
2. Current SCA educational framework is a teaching benchmark; it does not certify a self-authored CherryEdu course.
3. Software documentation explains what the instrument and processing algorithm **compute**, not which cup tastes best.
4. Equipment manuals and local safety rules outrank general roasting examples when procedures and emergencies are involved.
5. The real learner must distinguish **direct observation**, **instrument reading**, **inference**, **preference**, and **safety disposition**.

### Critical revisions in the original Roaster path

**Replace:** “DTR 12–18% is a precision ideal.”
**With:** DTR is time after first crack divided by total time, provided first crack can be defined and measured consistently; sensory results and degree of roast require other measures.

**Replace:** “Natural must be charged 10–15°C lower to protect sugar coating.”
**With:** Postharvest processes may correlate with physical or chemical differences in actual lots; neither the process label nor origin yields a universal charge temperature correction.

**Replace:** “High-density coffee necessarily needs higher charge.”
**With:** Density is one observed property among batch mass, specific roaster design, preheat, moisture, airflow and target product; establish safe local comparison before changing heat plan.

**Replace:** “Water activity 0.5–0.6 guarantees safe green beans.”
**With:** Moisture and aw have different definitions; food-safety clearance requires defined sampling and context-specific risk review, with suspicious material held by QC.

**Replace:** “A smooth descending RoR graph proves good coffee.”
**With:** Derivative curves are sensor- and processing-dependent; compare raw/event data, roast degree and properly controlled sensory results. Cosmetic smoothing does not roast beans.

**Replace:** “Underdeveloped always tastes sour; baked always tastes flat.”
**With:** Sensory defects are hypotheses that should be tested against preparation, coffee identity, roast profile and relevant reference controls.

## Cases requiring owner or equipment-specific signoff

- Machine emergency routines, chaff/fire response, interlocks, servicing and shutdown procedure.
- Accept/reject policy for moisture, aw, green defects and contamination.
- Actual machine capacities, calibrated probes, temperature units, valid profile change ranges and exhaust system.
- Cupping reference protocol (current SCA-102/103/104 where relevant) and panel training.
- Release/hold permissions, traceability, product labeling and claims.
- Evidence that actual learners understand conditions beyond recognizing a correct multiple-choice answer.

**Audit principle:** A teaching example with invented numbers is explicitly *simulated*, never passed off as an actual roast or cupping result.
