**Created:** 2026-10-12  
**Review type:** Internal desk review with cited source anchors. **Not an external scientific review or expert approval.**  
**Scope:** Five lessons, ten module-exit items, one applied QC/safety memo.
**Status:** COMPLETE AS AUTHORING DRAFT; **NOT cleared for production**.

## 1. Why five lessons?

The former Module 04 specification had **4** lessons: physical change, chemistry, roast level and degassing. It lacked an explicit practice tying the material to **reading roast logs, evidence-limited defect interpretation and roastery workplace safety**.

SCA's own Roasting Foundation scope covers roasting changes, basic profiles and safety, including machine maintenance/fire prevention; therefore these concerns belong in the learner's basic conceptual map. CherryEdu nevertheless remains an **online overview**, *not* an SCA Roasting course, a trainer-supervised equipment class, an operator license or equivalent evidence of hands-on skills.

New **M04.L05** introduces a beginner-safe analysis task (review a fictional roast log, identify missing evidence and escalate safety issues). No burner, airflow or machine maintenance instructions are given.

**Foundation architecture:** 7 modules; **34** lesson slots = M01 3 + M02 5 + M03 7 + M04 5 + M05 5 + M06 4 + M07 5. **History M07.L01 is mandatory and preserved.**

## 3. Evidence register with actual claim-to-source traceability

| Claim cluster | Source and type | What is grounded | Scope limitation / status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Roasting includes loss of water and gases, expansion, porosity/fragility and color change | [Pittia et al. 2001](https://doi.org/10.1006/fstl.2000.0749) experiment; [Indonesian Lampung Robusta study 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6365344/) experiment | Structural / physicochemical responses under specific roast conditions | **SOURCE SUPPORTED**; percentages, temperature and timing NOT generalized; technical SME pending |
| Heat transfer modes in drum/fluid-bed roasting | [Thermal Contaminants review 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10138461/) review | Conduction, convection, radiation and machine variations | **SOURCE SUPPORTED**; no universal fixed heat-transfer percentages |
| Maillard does not cease after first crack; stages are practical profile intervals | [Dynamics of titratable acidity 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11002029/) experimental paper | Explicitly recognizes continued Maillard chemistry beyond first crack | **SOURCE SUPPORTED**; avoids numerical “phase chemistry” divisions |
| Titratable acidity changes with roast profile, not single universal direction | [Dynamics of titratable acidity 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11002029/) | Changes under experimentally varied profiles, chemical metric TA | **SOURCE SUPPORTED**; not direct universal sensory acidity, pH or liking |
| Light/medium/dark are not complete quality grades; color can be measured via Agtron/CIELAB | [SCA The Color in Your Cup (2025)](https://sca.coffee/sca-news/25/issue-17/the-color-in-your-cup-roast-level-and-brew-temperature-significantly-affect-the-color-of-brewed-coffee-m7b3x) expert/research explainer | Difference between whole-bean and ground color; measurement approaches | **SOURCE SUPPORTED**; color scale values differ by method; avoid universal drop temps |
| Possible roast defects need sensory and chemical evidence, not a sole DTR/RoR number | [Giacalone et al. 2018](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0950329318302015) sensory and consumer experiment; [Yang et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4914823/) chemical markers | Experimental discrimination of deliberately constructed roast profiles | **SOURCE SUPPORTED with context**; one origin/controlled setups, no universal recipe or defect definition |
| CO2 produced during roasting, degassing in storage/grinding/extraction | [Time-Resolved Gravimetric Method (2017)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29091435/) instrument study | Gas release measured over time and different product states | **SOURCE SUPPORTED**; bloom intensity not a validated quality metric |
| Oxygen, packaging and repeated exposure can affect roast freshness | [Smrke et al. 2022](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2214289422000850) experimental packaging study; [SCA staling review 2012](https://sca.coffee/sca-news/2012/02/15/what-is-the-shelf-life-of-roasted-coffee-a-literature-review-on-coffee-staling-bjcx7) | Differences in studied aroma indices between four after-opening closure methods | **SOURCE SUPPORTED**; one trial result does not rank every package or fix a universal shelf-life |
| Roast log is production data, not direct proof of cup quality; RoR is a derived probe signal | [SCA Roasting Course Scope](https://education.sca.coffee/coffee-skills-program); [Roast-profile acidity 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11002029/) | Use of profile and contextual links to chemical outcomes | **CONCEPTUALLY GROUNDED**; instrument signal quirks need expert verification |
| Coffee roasting/grinding workplaces may expose workers to respiratory hazards including diacetyl, 2,3-pentanedione | [CDC/NIOSH 2016](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/bulletin/2016/coffee-workers.html) occupational bulletin | Documented risks and sources in investigated industrial workplaces | **SOURCE SUPPORTED**; cannot apply measured exposure levels to all roasting facilities or home use |
| SCA Roasting Foundation includes safety and basic machine practice | [SCA Coffee Skills Program](https://education.sca.coffee/coffee-skills-program) official curriculum | Specialist Foundation teaches basics and requires appropriate instruction | **VERIFIED OFFICIAL SCOPE**; CherryEdu is distinct, no SCA affiliation claimed |

### Disallowed claims

- “Maillard begins only at 150°C and ends at first crack.”
- “First crack occurs at exactly 195°C on every machine.”
- “A 12–18% or 20% DTR guarantees sweet, well-developed coffee.”
- “One smooth RoR curve proves no baked roast defect or predicts cup score.”
- “Dark roast is necessarily scorched/low quality, light roast always high quality.”
- “Rest espresso exactly 10 days; filter exactly 3 days, universally.”
- “One-way valve = permanent oxygen-free package.”
- “Roaster temperature graph proves occupational safety; inexperienced staff can fix exhaust or burner from a web lesson.”

## 4. Assessment desk QA

**Item distribution:** 10 items: 2 retrieval; 4 application; 3 evidence interpretation; 1 combined critique. Correct responses D-A-B-C-B-D-C-A-D-B. Keys have explanations and remediation target.

**Safety gate:** M04-Q09 and M04-Q10 are critical misconception items. In a real application, failing safety requires remediation independent of overall grade.

**Applied rubric:** 4 dimensions × 0–3 each (12 max), provisional 9/12 and no component <2. Measures *interpretation of paper logs only*, not actual operation.

**Validity warnings:**
- Single best answer must be checked for distractor ambiguity by actual SMEs.
- Simulated sensor values are not measurements.
- 10 MCQs do not certify roaster safety competence, professional skill, chemical analysis or tasting calibration.
- Existing CherryEdu quiz engine may not support rubric and variants; integration still to be specified.

## 5. Learner accessibility and creative delivery

All text is provided without requiring live burner, hot equipment, roast color meter or samples. **No-equipment route** is valid for interpreting concepts, not physical skills.

Suggested future educational media only if they add explanatory value:
1. Side-by-side photo series from actual green to roasted beans with accurate labeled stages (rights cleared; no fictional scientific photo).
2. Overlay showing **simultaneous** water loss, Maillard chemistry and gas development (do not animate non-existent cutoff temperatures).
3. Interactive roast-log screenshot highlighting difference between **sensor reading**, **annotation**, and **claim**; an accessible nonvisual data table is mandatory.
4. Simple visual model of degassing vs oxidative staling and packaging barrier, with clearly qualitative timing.

Avoid stock-filler images, AI-generated false instruments, emoji decoration or templated artificial enthusiasm. Assess time and confusion in a novice pilot.

## 7. Required next steps

1. Have a roasting SME check wording on thermal measurement, first crack, color, defect and practices. Require corrections where inconsistent with actual instrumentation.
2. Have occupational hygiene/safety specialist check worker exposure/ventilation wording and any machine safety scenario before learners see it.
3. Pilot concept comprehension with a small group of beginners; measure whether they mistakenly treat DTR or rest guidelines as absolute rules.
4. Calibrate question difficulty, ambiguity and the “safety-critical” gate in a controlled pilot.
5. Do not merge or mutate production until progress migration, test and approval gates pass.
